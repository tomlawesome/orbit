Reading your own meter

You can submit readings any time at my.haverfield-energy.co.uk or through the Haverfield Energy app. A photo of the meter dial helps us resolve any query quickly. If your meter has more than one register (Economy 7 or similar), please give both readings, and note whether your electricity meter reads in kWh or in units that need converting.

Smart meters send us readings automatically so your bills are based on what you actually use, not an estimate. Our installer, Fielden Install, is fitting smart meters across the network at no charge. If you're not sure whether yours is smart, check the display for a home screen showing today's spend, or ask when you next call 0800 112 2901.

Priority Services Register

If you or someone in your household is of pensionable age, disabled, chronically ill, or would otherwise benefit from extra support (for example advance notice of planned power cuts), you can join our free Priority Services Register. Registering also means our network partner, Kestrel Grid Networks, can prioritise your address during an unplanned outage.

Ways to use less energy

  • Turn appliances off standby rather than leaving them idle overnight.
  • Batch-wash at 30 degrees where you can — most modern detergents don't need higher.
  • Bleed radiators each autumn so the system doesn't work harder than it needs to.
  • Draught-proof letterboxes, keyholes and loft hatches; small gaps add up.

Understanding your tariff information label

Ofgem requires every domestic energy supplier to publish a Tariff Information Label alongside any summary or bill so you can compare rates on a like-for-like basis. Your label shows the unit rate and standing charge for both fuels, the estimated annual cost for a typical household, and whether the tariff is fixed, variable or tracks the default price cap. Your current label is for tariff code HAV-FIX-26-V3 and is available in full at hav.energy/tariffs.

Fixed versus variable tariffs

A fixed tariff, like the one on this account, locks your unit rates and standing charges for the length of the term shown above — in your case, a 12 month fixed term ending 30 September 2027. A variable tariff instead moves in line with the Ofgem default price cap, which is reviewed every three months. Some households prefer the certainty of a fixed tariff; others prefer the flexibility of moving supplier at any time without an exit fee. Your exit fee of £30.00 per fuel only applies if you leave before your fixed term ends.

What happens when your fixed term ends

Around 42 days before your tariff ends on 30 September 2027, we'll write to you with your renewal options, including any new fixed tariffs we're offering and what would happen if you did nothing. If you don't choose a new tariff, we'll move you onto our standard variable tariff, which tracks the Ofgem price cap and can be cancelled at any time without an exit fee. We'd always recommend comparing our renewal offer against the rest of the market, including through independent services such as switchwise.uk, before you decide.

How the price cap works

Ofgem sets a price cap on the unit rates and standing charges that suppliers can charge customers on default variable tariffs. The cap is not a cap on your total bill — if you use more energy, you pay more. It was last reviewed on 1 July 2026 and is reviewed every three months thereafter. Fixed tariffs, including yours, are not directly limited by the cap, though suppliers generally price them with reference to it.

Our commitment to vulnerable customers

We follow Ofgem's rules on supporting customers in vulnerable circumstances, including those on our Priority Services Register. This includes never disconnecting a Priority Services Register customer for debt during winter months without exhausting all other options first, and offering additional support with meter reading, billing formats and repayment plans. If cost is a worry, please contact us before a bill becomes overdue — we have a range of repayment plans and can check whether you're eligible for the Warm Home Discount, worth £150.00 this year, or other government schemes.

Switching supplier

You're free to switch supplier at any time under Ofgem's rules, and switching should not cause a loss of supply. If you're within your fixed term, your new supplier or switchwise.uk will usually tell you about any exit fee before you commit. Most switches complete within three weeks under the industry's reliable switching guarantee, which also covers your right to a "cooling off" period after signing up with a new supplier in case you change your mind.

Keeping your details up to date
