The landlord's duty — small print

Annual check. Regulation 36 of the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 requires a landlord to have every gas appliance and flue in a rented property checked for safety by a Gas Safe registered engineer within 12 months of installation and at intervals of no more than 12 months thereafter. This record is valid for 12 months from the date of inspection shown above, and the landlord is responsible for arranging the next check in good time.

Copies to tenants. A copy of this record, or the relevant parts of it, must be given to each existing tenant of the property within 28 days of the check, and to any new tenant before they move in. A copy has been provided as recorded in the signature block above.

Record retention. The landlord must retain this record, and the two preceding records where they exist, for at least 2 years from the date of this check. Records should be kept safely and produced on request to the local authority or to the Health and Safety Executive.

Access for the check. A landlord may need to take reasonable steps, including legal advice, to obtain access to the property if a tenant unreasonably refuses entry for the annual check. Refusal by a tenant does not remove the landlord's duty to have the check carried out.

Scope of this record. This record covers only the gas appliances, flues and pipework owned by the landlord and present at the property on the date of inspection. It does not cover appliances owned and installed by the tenant, gas supply pipework beyond the meter which is the supplier's responsibility, or electrical safety.

Checking this engineer. The engineer's Gas Safe registration can be checked using the licence number shown above. A registered engineer must carry a Gas Safe ID card and should be asked to show it before work begins.
