Guidance notes for the tenant

These notes explain what the attached notice means and what you can do about it. They are provided as general guidance only and are not a substitute for independent advice, which you can obtain free of charge from a local Citizens Advice office or a solicitor specialising in housing law.

What the notice means

Your tenancy has moved from a fixed term into a periodic tenancy running month to month. Because there is no fixed term left to run, the landlord can propose a new rent using the notice procedure described overleaf rather than by agreeing a new fixed-term agreement with you. The new rent set out in the notice will apply automatically from the date given unless you refer the matter to the tribunal in time and the tribunal sets a different figure.

The tribunal route and time limits

You have the right to apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) to determine the rent that a landlord might reasonably be expected to obtain for the property under a new periodic tenancy. Any application must be made before the date the proposed rent takes effect. Based on the dates on this notice, an application would need to reach the tribunal no later than 30 November 2026. If you do nothing by that date, the new rent shown on the notice takes effect automatically. The tribunal cannot normally set a rent higher than the one proposed in the notice.

Applying to the tribunal does not end your tenancy and the tribunal’s decision is binding on both you and the landlord for the period it covers. There is a fee to apply, and further guidance and the application form are available from the tribunal’s own website rather than from Quenby Fold Lettings.
